      On remand from my precedential Decision vacating the Board’s prior
institution in the above-referenced proceeding (Paper 20, “Director Review
Decision”), the Board issued a second Decision granting institution of inter
partes review (Paper 30, “Institution Decision”). For the reasons provided
below, Director Review of the Board’s Decision is initiated, the Decision
granting institution is vacated, and the Petition is denied.1
      In this case, Revvo Technologies, Inc. (“Petitioner”) did not propose
its own constructions for claim terms in the Petition, but instead accepted
Patent Owner’s proposed constructions submitted in the parties’ district
court litigation. Paper 1 (“Pet.”) at 9–10 (citing Ex. 1017, Exhibit A at 5–7,
10, Exhibit B at 10–15). Although Petitioner proposed different
constructions for the claim terms in the district court litigation, Petitioner
failed to explain why it was taking a different position here. Id. Petitioner
did argue, however, that its Petition complied with 37 C.F.R. § 42.104(b)(3)2
because Petitioner had adopted Patent Owner’s proposed constructions from
the district court litigation. See Paper 10 at 17, n.5.
      I initiated sua sponte Director Review and determined that the Board
had erred in limiting its consideration of a petitioner’s differing claim
construction positions to instances that implicate means-plus-function
interpretation under 35 U.S.C. § 112. Director Review Decision 3. I also
determined that the Board erred when it found that Petitioner had complied
with Rule 42.104(b)(3). In clarifying that a petitioner taking different claim

      I also explained that Petitioner has not chosen “a single claim
construction that best captures the true meaning of the patent claim,” as the
claim construction rules state, because Petitioner had proposed different
claim constructions as between the two forums. Id. (quoting Claim
Construction Standard, 83 Fed. Reg. at 51,350). Because I issued my
decision to clarify how a petitioner should approach different claim
construction positions and how the Office should assess those positions, I
then remanded this case to the Board to determine whether Petitioner had
provided a sufficient reason why its different claim construction positions
are warranted. Id.
      In further briefing on remand, Petitioner maintained that it had
proposed broader claim constructions because the district court may decline
to adopt the narrow constructions that it has pursued in the litigation. Paper
23, 1. Petitioner asserted that because claim construction in the litigation “is
not scheduled to occur for several months,” it could not have filed a timely

        The Board found Petitioner’s explanation sufficient “because it
follows” my “exemplary logic” that a district court “might in the future
decline to adopt constructions that a petitioner proposes in related district
court litigation.” Institution Decision 8. It is not. This is error and
mischaracterizes both my decision and its import. My “exemplary logic” is
thus: in the generally rare instances where diverging constructions are
advanced before two different tribunals, a party may proffer a different,
broader construction before the Board when the district court already has
rejected petitioner’s narrower construction. Director Review Decision 4;
accord Sun Pharm. Indus. Inc. v. Nivagen Pharm., Inc., IPR2025-00893,
Paper 18 at 3 (Director Sept. 19, 2025) (informative). Petitioner’s
explanation here amounts to nothing more than an assertion that a petitioner
should be permitted to raise different claim constructions in the two forums
when there is a chance that the district court will reject that petitioner’s
preferred construction. My precedential decision does not provide for
conditional future outcomes, and while petitioner would no doubt like to
‘wait and see,’ we cannot and will not. The appropriate course of action
under these circumstances is to deny institution.
