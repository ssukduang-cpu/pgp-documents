      Brook & Whittle Ltd. (Patent Owner) filed a request for
discretionary denial (Paper 7, DD Req.) in the above-captioned case, and
Multi-Color Corporation (Petitioner) filed an opposition (Paper 9, DD
Opp.).
      After considering the parties arguments and the record, and in view
of all relevant considerations, discretionary denial of institution is not
appropriate in this proceeding. This determination is based on the totality of
the evidence and arguments the parties have presented.
      Some factors weigh in favor of discretionary denial. For example, the
challenged patent is currently at issue in two district court proceedingsa
patent infringement suit in the Eastern District of Texas and a declaratory
judgment action (noninfringement) in the District of Delaware. DD Req. 1;
DD Opp. 5; Paper 2, 1; Paper 4, 2. The scheduled trial date in the Texas
proceeding is May 18, 2026, which is approximately four months before the
September 21, 2026, final written decision due date in this proceeding. DD
Req. 12. As such, it is unlikely that a final written decision in this
proceeding will issue before the Texas district court trial occurs.
      Other factors counsel against discretionary denial. For example,
Petitioner is not a party to either proceeding, and represents that it has no
substantive relationship with any defendant in the district court proceedings.
DD Opp. 4. Further, no trial date has been scheduled in the parallel
proceeding in Delaware. Id. at 11. In addition, the challenged patent issued
on April 16, 2024, and Petitioner filed its Petition on January 16, 2025
nine months from the grant of the challenged patent. Petitions for post-grant
review are favored because they must be filed no later than nine months
from the grant of the patent (35 U.S.C. § 321(c)), are close in time to
examination, and occur before expectations in the patent rights are strongly
